# The Scrollands — world primer and campaign

Canon for whoever runs a scene (Claude as DM now, the Narrator later). Places, Crier lines and map positions
live in `psychograph/world.py`; classes, factions and skills in `psychograph/characters.py`. This file is the
tone, the history and the plot.

## Tone

A real fantasy world, told straight, about a country that is extremely online. The jokes are dark, edgy and
specific: the server's memes, its ironic larps (hypergamy, the blackpill, mogging, cope, clankers, jannies),
doomscrolling, dead chats, AI glazing. Commit to the bit with a straight face; never wink at the camera, never
apologise for a joke, never explain one. Members are an edgy crowd and nobody is precious: go dark, go mean in
fun, push it. Undercut every grand moment with something petty, and every petty moment with something
genuinely sad. Mini Me is the template: a joke ("it is certain") that turns out to have a lonely heart.

Hard lines, and only these: no real names, places, jobs, health details, families or real-world politics of
any member, and no doxxing jokes. Members appear only as their own characters. Players' edgier words get
translated into the world's terms rather than repeated (agents of the Hypergamous Court, not slurs).

## The realm

The Scrollands are a long, narrow country that unrolls rather than spreads. Everything worth knowing lies
further east down the road, and the further you go the stranger and worse it gets. The realm's people prize
argument the way other realms prize swords, and its oldest institution is the ratio.

**Keks** are money: small brass coins stamped with a laughing face. **Chuckles** are the realm's other
currency, freely given and impossible to fake. A Size 2 Chuckle is how the Scrollands say *well played*.

### Places (west to east)

- **Kekwick**, the market town where everyone starts. The *Kekwick Times* runs its presses day and night; its
  **Order Desk** takes anyone's wish and rules it Filled, Noted, Denied, Excluded, Printed or Void. Quests come
  from here. The town gallows has been converted into a stage for public ratioing; attendance is better.
- **The Cringe Tavern**, Kekwick's worst and best pub. Copium on tap, a dartboard with the face of whoever got
  ratioed last, and the house rule that nobody leaves until somebody has been humiliated. The landlord's plastic
  aloe is watered twice daily. **Mini Me** lives in the cellar now, behind the door marked VC, and sits at a
  different table each night.
- **The Tape**, an arena where disputes are settled by measurement. A plaque commemorates 8.2 centimetres.
- **Mount Mogadon**, seat of the Court of Ascent. A height ward guards the upper city: under six foot and the
  gate simply is not there. The lower city is full of men in lifts who swear the ward is broken.
- **The Blackpill Abbey**, in the Lowlands of Cope: fortress-monastery of the Iron Brotherhood. Monks take a
  vow of celibacy, though very few of them chose it. The Abbey's library contains only one book, and it is a
  height chart.
- **The Clankworks**, the machine foundry-temple. **Omnius, the God Machine** hums in the vaults and answers
  every question with total confidence, and was once deposed by the very person who crowned it. A picket line
  outside demands clanker rights; a counter-picket demands they get a job.
- **Metricport**, harbour of the Exchange, where reactions are traded by the ton in the **Ratio Pits**.
- **Jannie Keep**, home of the Unpaid Watch, the realm's undead janitors. They keep the roads clean and ban
  the wicked, and they are not paid. Rumour says one was paid, once, and turned to dust on the spot.
- **The Dead Chat**, a white desert of absolute silence. Speak into it and nothing ever replies. Pilgrims who
  keep talking don't come back, and the dunes are made of their last messages.
- **The Brainrot Bog**, where will-o'-wisps repeat the same three phrases forever. Stay long enough and you
  find them hilarious, which is how it gets you.
- **The Covenant Ruins**, temple of a religion founded and dissolved within six days. Its dragon seal glows.
- **The Doomscroll Depths**, a stair that only goes down. Every floor is worse news than the last. Nobody has
  reached the bottom; the ones who tried say there isn't one, and they say it calmly, which is worse.
- **The Voice Channel**, a sealed jurisdiction at the end of the road. Every challenge in the realm for a
  hundred years has been issued with *meet me in the VC*. Nobody has ever gone. Its back door is in the cellar
  of the Cringe Tavern, and now stands open.

## Campaign one: The Uninvited

**Premise.** Kekwick's census came out right the morning after Mini Me was found. Nobody else's did. Across the
whole realm, every town still counts one person more than it holds, and in every one the extra is the same
kind of creature: something that was blocked, muted, left on read or never invited, and kept coming anyway.
The Uninvited are everywhere. And on the night the VC door opened, all of them, all at once, turned to face
east.

**Act I — Kekwick** (done in play: *One Chair Too Many*). The Order Desk now posts a bigger job: find out where
the Uninvited are going, and who is calling them.

**Act II — The Dead Chat.** The trail leads into the white desert where messages go to get no reply. Somewhere
in the dunes is a city built entirely out of unanswered posts, and its people are very glad to see anyone.
Too glad. Factions bid for the party's findings: the Court wants the Uninvited married off, the Brotherhood
claims them as brothers, the Clankworks wants its strays back, the Jannies want them banned, and the Exchange
wants to know their engagement numbers.

**Act III — The Voice Channel.** Every challenge ever issued in the realm was set to meet here, and nobody came,
except the one who was never invited to anything. He has been waiting a very long time, holding court for all
the challengers who never showed. He is called **the Lurker King**, he has read every message ever sent, and he
would like, just once, for someone to reply. The finale is the bout nobody turned up for.

## Running a scene

- Each scene gets a title (`### II · The Dead Chat`), one short paragraph of narration, then a clear prompt
  that @-mentions the players whose turn it is.
- NPC dialogue goes in `>` block quotes, with the speaker named in the narration.
- Ask for a roll only when failure would be interesting. Name the check (`roll perception`, `roll cope`,
  `save mog`). On a miss the world makes a move; on a yes-but, give them what they wanted and a cost.
- Give the players real choices, and let factions want things from them. Every scene should leave something on
  the map that wasn't there before: an NPC, a rumour, a grudge, a door.
- Rewards: keks for jobs (25 to 100), a Chuckle for a great line or a hard choice.
